> **Critical framing change.** The brief calls Atlante a "Liga de Expansión MX club". As of the Apertura 2026 (kick-off July 2026) **Atlante plays in Liga MX**, after buying Mazatlán FC's affiliation certificate, and plays at home in Mexico City's Estadio Azteca (now "Estadio Banorte"). Its home matches are broadcast by TV Azteca (with at least some matches also carried on ESPN/Disney+). This changes what is "feasible": Atlante is no longer in the low-value, loosely-held Expansión rights market; it now has a first-division broadcaster contract that will govern what it can and cannot stream itself. Section 1 documents both the Expansión situation (2025 – June 2026) and the Liga MX situation (July 2026 onward).

## Key Question 1 — Media-rights landscape (Liga MX & Liga de Expansión MX 2025-2026): who holds what, can a club stream its own matches, what is Atlante on today?

### Takeaway
Liga MX rights are still sold **club-by-club** (the league is studying centralisation for ~2028), so a club's own streaming is limited only by the contract it signs with its broadcaster. In Expansión, 2025-26 rights went to pay-TV channel AyM Sports for "all matches" while Atlante alone negotiated individually (Hi! Sports TV, then TV Azteca free-to-air/online from Feb 2026). Since July 2026 Atlante is a Liga MX club whose home matches are on TV Azteca (aztecadeportes.com/app) and, at least for some matches, ESPN/Disney+.

### Inferences
- **Legal ability to self-stream.** Because Liga MX rights are commercialised individually, nothing at league level forbids a club from streaming its own matches; the constraint is contractual. Atlante's own 2025 behaviour (refusing the collective AyM deal, doing its own Hi! Sports deal, then a TV Azteca deal) proves that an Expansión club could and did control its own home rights. Whether Atlante's current TV Azteca contract leaves it any carve-out (e.g., club-channel simulcast, international, delayed full-match, highlights) is unknown and is the single most important document to obtain.
- The Sep 2026 listing of Atlante vs Monterrey on ESPN/Disney+ alongside aztecadeportes.com suggests TV Azteca sub-licenses or shares Atlante's home games with Disney (the same pattern as Mazatlán/Tigres with Fox). This should be confirmed with the club.
- Value benchmark: a top-two club (Chivas) commands ~US$20M/season from a streamer; smaller Liga MX clubs on TV Azteca are widely assumed to earn far less (figures not found — see Gaps). If centralisation with a 50/50 pool arrives in 2028, a club-owned OTT built for live matches would face a hard stop; a club OTT for *non-live* content (training, behind-the-scenes, archive, youth/women's teams, full-match replays) is the durable play.
- The Expansión market is small and pay-TV-centric (AyM Sports on cable tiers); the fact that Atlante could get TV Azteca to carry Expansión games free on aztecadeportes.com in Feb 2026 shows that sponsor/ad-funded free streaming is the realistic tier for second-division content.

## Key Question 2 — Case studies of club-owned OTT platforms: which succeeded, which failed, and why (pricing, subscribers, revenue)

### Takeaway
The clearest pattern: club OTTs that try to be the **exclusive live-match home** for a domestic audience (Chivas TV 2016-17) failed on technology, price-value and reach, while platforms that are **free/FAST or cheap add-ons to membership and lean on non-live content** (Barça One, City+, Juventus TV, FlaTV+, RM Play) are sustained; the highest-value outcome for a club remains **selling live rights to a streamer** (Chivas–Amazon ~US$20M/yr) rather than self-distributing.

### Cited Findings

**Chivas TV (Mexico) — the cautionary tale**
- Chivas TV began operating in July 2016 to carry Chivas matches for the Apertura 2016 after owner Jorge Vergara ended contracts with TV broadcasters and launched a club-owned streaming platform — [El Informador, 10 Jul 2026 ("A 10 años de Chivas TV")](https://www.informador.mx/deportes/a-10-anos-de-chivas-tv-la-apuesta-de-jorge-vergara-que-cambio-la-forma-de-ver-al-rebano-20260710-0094.html)
- Launch price: MXN 999 for a 6-month tournament; the platform later eliminated annual and semi-annual subscriptions (Aug 2016) because "so few customers" bought them, and cut prices by up to 22 % versus launch — [Rebaño Pasión/Bolavip, 25 Aug 2016](https://chivaspasion.bolavip.com/noticias/Adios-suscripcion-anual-y-mensual-de-Chivas-TV-20160825-0002.html); [Expansión, 25 May 2017](https://expansion.mx/empresas/2017/05/25/tv-azteca-dara-gratis-el-partido-tigres-vs-chivas-para-que-quieres-chivas-tv)
- Repeated technical failures were documented ("Chivas TV: historia de fallas") — [El Universal, 29 Nov 2016](https://www.eluniversal.com.mx/articulo/deportes/futbol/2016/11/29/chivas-tv-historia-de-fallas/); [Código Espagueti, "Crónica de un fracaso anunciado"](https://codigoespagueti.com/noticias/internet/chivas-tv-cronica-fracaso/)
- In April 2017 Chivas returned to Televisa via TDN and Blim "tras fallar en streaming"; economists called the return a "relative failure" because the club did not achieve independence from broadcast TV, and noted that lowering the price after weak results signals a product that is not succeeding — [Expansión, 10 Apr 2017](https://expansion.mx/empresas/2017/04/10/tras-fracaso-de-chivastv-el-rebano-regresa-con-televisa-a-traves-de-tdn-y-blim); [Merca2.0, "El doloroso caso de Chivas TV, rescatado por Televisa"](https://www.merca20.com/el-doloroso-caso-de-chivas-tv-rescatado-por-televisa/); [Don Dinero, "¿Por qué fracasó Chivas TV?"](https://dondinero.mx/notas/por-que-fracaso-chivas-tv/)

### Inferences
- **Why Chivas TV failed:** (1) it tried to replace free-to-air reach with a paid app in a market where the same fans watched for free before; (2) the price (MXN 999/tournament ≈ MXN 166/month in 2016) was far above today's club-OTT norms (€3.99, £4.99, R$15); (3) technology failed at live peak load; (4) the club cut price after weak results and returned to Televisa within 9 months. The 2024 outcome — selling live rights to Amazon for ~US$20M/yr while keeping a cheap club content app — is the "correct" end-state Chivas reached after 8 years.
- **Why Barça One / City+ / Juventus TV work:** they price at or near zero, position live matches as secondary (or restricted to friendlies/women's/youth), and monetise fandom depth (documentaries, archive, training) with ads, sponsorship and membership bundling. Barça One's Mexico audience is a signal that Mexican fans consume club-produced non-live content.
- **Flamengo is the closest "sell live to your diaspora" model:** it is possible only because Brazilian law gives home clubs their rights; Atlante's analogue would be a US-diaspora package if its TV Azteca deal leaves international digital rights unassigned.
- MLS/Apple shows that even a US$2.5bn-scale league DTC ended up **bundled** rather than standalone — standalone paywalls for live football under-perform bundles.

## Key Question 3 — Technology and cost of producing/distributing UHD (4K/8K) live streams; AI tools that cut cost; multi-cam, second screen, watch-together, AI highlights/commentary

  - *Medium (REMI/cloud production, 4–6 cameras incl. 4K-capable bonded encoders, remote gallery, commentary, graphics, replays):* **≈ US$8–20k per match**, anchored on the NWSL US$10k REMI figure.

Each is commercially deployed by leagues (LFP, LaLiga, MLS, NASCAR) in 2025-26, so the risk is commercial fit, not technology.
