## KQ1. What would it cost to create and run 1–3 AI avatar influencers for a club, and what returns have others seen?

### Takeaway
The best-documented commercial AI influencer (Aitana López, The Clueless, Barcelona) earns roughly €1,000+ per sponsored post and ~€3,000/month on average (peaks ~€10,000/month) with ~300K Instagram followers reached in about six months; tooling costs are now low (avatar-video SaaS from ~US$29/month, API video at ~US$3/minute), so the dominant cost is the creative/community team, not software. No source found gives a full "cost to build and operate" breakdown for a sports club; returns for clubs are undocumented.

### Inferences
- Software cost for 1–3 avatars is trivial relative to labour: a HeyGen/Synthesia seat (~US$30–50/month each) plus ElevenLabs voice (~US$22/month) and an image model subscription puts tooling at well under US$200/month per avatar; at ~US$3/minute API video, 100 minutes/month of avatar video is ~US$300. The real budget line is 1–2 creative/community staff (or an agency retainer) to script, generate, QA, post and moderate.
- Aitana's economics (~€1,000/post, ~€3K/month average) are for a general-lifestyle persona with 300K+ followers built over ~2 years by a specialised agency; a club avatar would more realistically monetise through sponsor integrations (a sponsor "adopts" the avatar) and content-cost avoidance than through third-party paid posts.
- The wide discrepancy in Lil Miquela revenue estimates (US$2M/yr vs US$74K on IG) shows that third-party earnings figures for virtual influencers are unreliable; only creator-disclosed numbers (The Clueless) should be used.

## KQ2. Which sports organizations already use AI avatars or virtual ambassadors, with results?

### Takeaway
Sports bodies have mostly deployed AI as conversational companions or licensed digital doubles of real athletes (Premier League/Microsoft Companion, Bundesliga "Captain", Houston Rockets "ClutchBot", NBPA–Genies avatars, Coca-Cola China's real-time Fan Zhiyi avatar for the 2026 World Cup) rather than invented AI "influencer" personas; the one high-profile invented persona in motorsport (Mahindra Racing's "Ava") was withdrawn after fan backlash. ## KQ3. What disclosure rules apply in Mexico and on Meta/TikTok for AI-generated personas?

### Takeaway
Meta and TikTok now both label AI content (Meta's "AI info" label via creator toggle or metadata detection; TikTok auto-labels via C2PA Content Credentials and requires labels on realistic AI people/scenes, and bans synthetic media of private individuals); the EU AI Act Article 50 makes chatbot and deepfake disclosure mandatory from 2 August 2026 (fines up to €15M/3% turnover). Mexico has no AI-specific law in force; several 2025 initiatives (notably a 30 April 2025 bill amending the Federal Criminal Code and Copyright Law to criminalise deceptive deepfakes) and state-level deepfake rules exist, and no PROFECO guidance specific to virtual influencers was found.

### Cited Findings
- Meta provides an "AI info" label that creators attach voluntarily plus automatic labelling "when its detection systems identify generated content"; Meta renamed the label from "Made with AI" to "AI info" to avoid labelling minor AI edits — [Influencer Marketing Hub, AI disclosure rules](https://influencermarketinghub.com/ai-disclosure-rules/); [PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/meta-changes-ai-generated-content-label_id160026)
- Meta expects disclosure of "photorealistic AI content; failing to disclose materially deceptive AI media can lead to penalties" — [Gradually.ai](https://www.gradually.ai/en/instagram-ai-label/); [Mintface, Instagram AI content policy 2026](https://mintface.ai/blog/instagram-ai-content-policy-2026)
- For ads, Meta applies the AI info label when ads are created/edited using its generative features (Background Generation, Image Generation, Add Animation) that "significantly modify" media, and also labels ads made with external tools (e.g., Photoshop, DALL-E) "when it detects relevant metadata" — [Coinis blog, 2026](https://coinis.com/blog/meta-ai-content-labeling-facebook-instagram-ads-2026); [ALM Corp](https://almcorp.com/news/meta-ai-labels-facebook-instagram-ads-disclosure-2026/)
- TikTok integrated C2PA Content Credentials to automatically detect and label AI content and "has since labeled over 1.3 billion AI-generated videos" — [Dynamoi](https://dynamoi.com/learn/ai-music-distribution/tiktok-ai-content-statistics); [Billo](https://billo.app/blog/ai-labeling/) (one snippet dates the C2PA integration to January 2025; TikTok's original C2PA announcement was in 2024 — verify)
- TikTok policy (2026) "requires visible labeling on all AI-generated visuals and audio that depict realistic people or scenes"; AI captions, hashtags, scripts are exempt; "deepfakes that impersonate real people without labeling are prohibited" and "synthetic media featuring real private individuals, even with a label, is banned entirely" — [Cinerads, TikTok AI policy 2026](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/tiktok-ai-content-policy); [AuditSocials](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/tiktok-ai-content-disclosure-rules-2026)
- EU AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations "apply from 2 August 2026"; the Commission published draft guidelines on 8 May 2026 and adopted final guidelines on 20 July 2026; chatbots must inform users of their non-human nature; deepfake-like content "must be labeled – even if no deception was intended and even if no real individual is depicted"; fines "up to EUR 15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover"; the AI Omnibus proposal may delay the Art. 50(2) marking/detection duties to 2 December 2026 (adoption pending) — [Greenberg Traurig, Jun 2026](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/6/deepfakes-chatbots-ai-generated-text-european-commission-details-transparency-obligations-under-the-ai-act); [European Commission guidelines](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-ai-transparency-obligations); [Stibbe](https://www.stibbe.com/publications-and-insights/the-ai-acts-transparency-obligations-rules-scope-and-timeline)
- Mexico: "On April 30, 2025, a legislative proposal was presented to reform various provisions of the Federal Criminal Code and Federal Copyright Law regarding generative artificial intelligence", introducing offences for "creating or spreading deepfakes that deceptively manipulate audio or video" and proposing copyright treatment of AI-assisted works — [Regulations.ai, RAI-MX-2025](https://regulations.ai/regulations/RAI-MX-NA-IQRDDXX-2025); several parallel initiatives summarised by [Instituto Autor](https://institutoautor.org/mexico-se-presentan-varias-iniciativas-legislativas-para-regular-la-inteligencia-artificial/)
- Mexican ad-tech analysis: the regulatory direction "is not on prohibiting AI, but on making transparent how it is used"; "labeling and transparency of synthetic content … will directly impact campaigns with virtual influencers, AI-generated ads" — [AdTech México](https://adtech.com.mx/ley-ia-mexico-adtech-madtech-marketing-digital/)
- State-level: San Luis Potosí has regulated deepfakes (analysis in Nexos) — [Nexos, El Juego de la Corte](https://eljuegodelacorte.nexos.com.mx/regulacion-de-deep-fakes-en-san-luis-potosi/); overview of which Mexican laws apply to deepfakes — [Sopitas](https://www.sopitas.com/noticias/deepfakes-inteligencia-artificial-grok-leyes-mexico/)
- Consumer trust: "52% of consumers distrust undisclosed virtual content" (Influencer Marketing Hub research, early 2025) — [Influencers-Time](https://www.influencers-time.com/virtual-influencers-in-2025-balancing-opportunity-and-threat/)

### Inferences
- A Mexican club posting AI-persona content on Instagram/Facebook/TikTok should always self-toggle the "AI info"/AIGC label and embed C2PA credentials; platforms will detect and label anyway, and undisclosed photorealistic personas risk penalties and reputational damage.
- Because Atlante's content reaches EU-resident fans, Article 50 duties (chatbot disclosure, deepfake labelling) will apply to any EU-facing chatbot/avatar from 2 Aug 2026 in practice, even if the club is Mexican.
- In Mexico the binding rules today are general: consumer-protection (Ley Federal de Protección al Consumidor, misleading advertising), likeness/image rights under the Copyright Law and civil law, Ley Olimpia-type criminal provisions for intimate deepfakes, and state deepfake statutes; an AI-specific federal statute is not yet in force per the sources found.

### Gaps
- No PROFECO guidance or enforcement action specific to virtual influencers or AI advertising was found (searches on PROFECO + influencers returned nothing relevant).
- Status (approved/pending) of the April 2025 federal bill and of the broader "Ley General de IA" initiatives as of September 2026 could not be verified (search budget exhausted).
- Could not read Meta's or TikTok's own policy pages; the rules above are from secondary guides.

## KQ5. What is WhatsApp Business API pricing in Mexico in 2025–2026, and which vendors are strongest in Mexico?

### Takeaway
Since 1 July 2025 Meta bills business-initiated template messages per message delivered (not per 24-hour conversation), keeps customer-service conversations free, and offers volume discounts up to ~20%; snippets give Mexico base rates of roughly US$0.030–0.044 per marketing message, US$0.008–0.0085 per utility message and ~US$0.021 per authentication message (BSPs add mark-ups), with a further Mexican marketing-rate cut noted. Meta-certified BSPs with strong Mexican presence include Auronix (Mexico City), Yalo (Mexico/LatAm conversational commerce), Botmaker, Gupshup (LatAm/India), Infobip and Twilio.

### Cited Findings
- Mexico "example base rates are $0.0436 for marketing, $0.0080 for utility and $0.0207 for authentication" — [EngageLab, WhatsApp Business API pricing 2026 (incl. ### Inferences
- For a mid-size Mexican club, the ranking by evidence strength and cost is: (1) WhatsApp Channel (free) + WhatsApp Business API with an AI agent for tickets/membership/service (utility messages at <US$0.01, service free); (2) automated highlights/vertical clips (WSC Sports or cheaper alternatives) — the only content use case with published engagement multiples; (3) data-driven ticket pricing (published +14% sales/+39% revenue at an Italian club), which needs a ticketing partner capable of variable pricing; (4) an AI avatar/mascot, which has no published ROI in sports and carries disclosure and backlash risk, so it should be treated as a low-budget experiment tied to a sponsor rather than a core investment.
